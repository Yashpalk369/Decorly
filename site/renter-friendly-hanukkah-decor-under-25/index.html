9 Renter-Friendly Hanukkah Decor Ideas Under $25

Rental-Friendly Decor November 17, 2026·2,704 words

Deposit-safe Hanukkah decor under $25 — flameless menorahs, removable hooks, blue-and-silver touches and window displays that come down without a mark.

Hanukkah 2026 runs from the evening of 14 December to the evening of 22 December, which gives you a full month to plan a display that feels festive without putting your deposit at risk. A rental flat is actually a lovely place to celebrate the Festival of Lights: eight nights of candles, blue and silver everywhere, and a room that glows a little warmer each evening as you add another flame. The trick is choosing decorations that come down in January without leaving a mark, a hole or a puddle of wax on the windowsill.

Everything in this roundup costs under $25, and most of it costs well under that. None of it needs nails, screws or drilling. Flameless candles replace real flames, removable hooks and clings replace pins and tape, and every piece is easy to put up, easy to take down and easy to pack away for next year. Budget around $60 to $80 for the whole look — or pick three or four favourites and have a beautiful, complete display for under $50.

These nine picks are the ones that actually work in rented rooms. They are small, light and affordable, and they look far more expensive than they are once the lights go down and the blue glow takes over.

1. Flameless LED Menorah

A flameless LED menorah is the single most important purchase in this whole list, and it solves the biggest renter problem at a stroke: real flames and rented flats do not mix well. Most tenancy agreements ban open flames outright, and even where they do not, a burning candle on a wooden windowsill while you are asleep is a worry nobody needs for eight nights. An LED menorah gives you the same warm glow and the same nightly ritual of adding a light, with none of the risk.

Choose one with a timer and warm-white LEDs rather than harsh blue-white ones; the timer handles the nightly schedule for you. A slim design in silver, brushed gold or wood-effect fits a narrow windowsill where a chunky brass menorah would never sit safely.

Place it in the window and it becomes the centrepiece of your whole display. Prices start around $12 for a simple battery model and run to about $22 for one with a timer and remote. buy on Amazon

2. Blue-and-Silver Window Clings

Window clings are the renter's best friend at festival time: they stick with static, not glue, so they peel off in January leaving absolutely nothing behind — no residue, no torn paint, no sticky shadow on the glass. A pack of blue-and-silver Hanukkah clings, with stars of David, dreidels, menorahs and candles, turns a plain window into a display in about five minutes, and it works from both sides, so passers-by get the show too.

Apply them to clean, dry glass and smooth out air bubbles by hand. When the festival ends, peel them off, stick them back on their backing sheet and store them flat — a decent set survives three or four Hanukkahs, which makes a $8 to $12 pack one of the best-value buys on this list.

Pair them with a string of lights behind for a beautiful after-dark glow. If you want more ideas for dressing a rental window without a single nail, our guide to hanging Christmas lights in a rental covers the same removable-hook tricks that work just as well for Hanukkah. buy on Amazon

3. Removable Star Garland

A garland is the fastest way to make a room feel decorated, and a star garland in blue, silver and white is the Hanukkah version of the classic paper chain. The trick in a rental is hanging it without damage: use small removable adhesive hooks spaced along the top of a wall or across a doorway, and drape the garland in a gentle swag rather than pulling it taut. Command-style hooks hold a lightweight paper or felt garland easily and come away without a trace when you are done.

Felt stars look soft in a cosy room; metallic foil ones catch the light and throw it around. A two-metre garland suits a doorway or the top of a bookshelf; a longer three-metre one spans a sofa wall or a mantelpiece. Layer two garlands together — one blue, one silver — for a fuller look that still costs under $20.

Hang it early and leave it up all eight nights. Most garlands cost between $7 and $15, and a felt one will pack flat for next year. buy on Amazon

4. Dreidel Table Scatter and Centrepiece

A dreidel scatter is the easiest centrepiece you will ever make: a handful of small dreidels, a scattering of chocolate gelt and a few metallic confetti stars spread down the middle of the table, and suddenly an ordinary dinner table looks like a celebration. It costs almost nothing, it takes two minutes to arrange, and it doubles as entertainment — guests can spin the dreidels while dinner cooks, and children love a game that comes with chocolate.

Use the largest dreidel from a mixed blue, silver and gold pack as the anchor in the centre. Surround it with a ring of gelt coins, then scatter smaller dreidels and stars outward so the display fades into the table rather than stopping abruptly.

This is also a lovely pick for a shared house, because it lives on the table rather than on the walls, so nobody has to negotiate over hooks or landlord rules. A pack of dreidels costs around $6 to $10, and the gelt doubles as dessert. buy on Amazon

5. Hanukkah Throw Pillow Covers

Throw pillow covers are the laziest decoration in this list, and that is a compliment. You do not hang anything, stick anything or light anything: you zip a blue-and-silver cover over the cushions you already own, and the sofa instantly joins the festival. Choose covers with a menorah, a dreidel or a simple Star of David pattern in navy, silver and white, and they will tie the whole room together — the garland, the window clings and the menorah all suddenly look like one deliberate scheme.

Covers are far better than whole pillows for a renter. They cost half as much, they fold flat into a drawer in January, and you are not stuck storing a pile of seasonal cushions in a flat that has no spare cupboard. A standard 18-inch square fits most sofa cushions; a set of two or four dresses the sofa and an armchair.

Pick soft cotton or linen-look fabric over shiny satin, which can look cheap in daylight. A set of two covers usually costs $12 to $18, and four-cover sets often come in under $25. buy on Amazon

6. Battery Fairy Lights in Blue and White

Fairy lights are the decoration that makes every other decoration work harder. A string of battery-powered lights in blue and white, draped along a bookshelf, wound through a garland or coiled inside a glass jar, gives your whole room a soft blue glow that no single candle can match — and because they run on batteries, there is no trailing cable, no socket hunt and no landlord conversation about extension leads.

We recommended the same approach for Christmas in our guide to battery Christmas candles for small apartments under $20, and the logic is identical here: the glow without the fire risk, and a timer that handles the switching off for you. Look for copper-wire strings, which bend easily around shelves and frames, and a warm-white-plus-blue mix rather than pure icy blue, which can feel cold in a small room.

Drape one behind the menorah, one along the bookshelf and one inside a jar on the table. A two-pack of 20-light strings with timers costs around $8 to $14. buy on Amazon

7. Gelt Jars as a Display

Gelt jars are decoration you can eat, which makes them the most practical pick on this list. Fill a couple of clear glass jars or apothecary-style canisters with chocolate gelt coins — gold foil, silver foil and blue-wrapped — and set them on a sideboard, a kitchen shelf or the dining table. They look like a deliberate display, they cost very little, and they quietly disappear over the eight nights as everyone helps themselves.

Use jars you already own — a mason jar or pasta jar works perfectly — and only buy the gelt. If you do buy jars, choose ones with a wide mouth so hands can reach in easily, and pick a couple of different heights so the display has some shape.

Layer the coins rather than tipping them in randomly: a band of gold, a band of silver and a band of blue makes the jar look designed rather than just full. A bag of gelt costs around $6 to $9, and two filled jars will carry you through the whole festival. buy on Amazon

8. Hanukkah Table Runner

A table runner is the one piece that makes every meal of the festival feel special, and it does it without a single hook, nail or adhesive strip. Lay a navy runner with silver stars, dreidels or a menorah motif down the centre of the dining table, add your dreidel scatter and a jar of fairy lights on top of it, and the table is dressed for all eight nights. When the festival ends, fold it up and put it in a drawer — no marks, no holes, nothing to repair.

Choose a runner in a washable fabric, because a dining table in December sees latkes, sufganiyot, hot chocolate and at least one spilled drink. Cotton and polyester blends wash easily and dry quickly, and a navy base hides the odd stain far better than white or cream. A 70 to 90 inch runner suits a standard four-to-six-seat table — measure yours first.

At $10 to $20, a runner is one of the best-value pieces here, and it is the one you will reach for again next year without a second thought. buy on Amazon

9. Star of David Wall Decals

Wall decals are the renter's answer to wall art: they go up in minutes, they look painted on from across the room, and they peel off without taking paint with them — as long as you buy the removable vinyl kind and not the permanent kind, so check the listing carefully before you order. A set of blue and silver Stars of David in a few sizes lets you build a feature wall above the sofa, frame a doorway, or create a small cluster behind the menorah display.

Plan the layout on the floor first, largest to smallest, then transfer them one at a time. A loose, scattered arrangement looks more natural than a rigid grid, and leaving a little breathing room between stars stops the wall feeling crowded. The same removable-decal trick works year-round — our guide to renter-friendly Christmas wall decals under $25 covers the same damage-free approach for December.

Test one decal in an inconspicuous spot first, leave it for a day, and peel it off to check your paint is happy before committing to the full wall. Most sets cost $8 to $15 and include enough stars for a whole feature wall. buy on Amazon

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a real menorah with candles in a rental flat?

It depends on your tenancy agreement, and you should check it before lighting anything. Many rental contracts ban open flames, and even where they do not, a burning candle on a windowsill is a fire risk in a small flat. A flameless LED menorah gives you the same nightly ritual and the same glow with none of the risk, and it is the option most landlords are comfortable with.

How do I hang Hanukkah decorations without damaging the walls?

Use removable adhesive hooks and static-cling decorations, and avoid nails, screws, pins and strong tape. Small adhesive hooks hold lightweight garlands and fairy lights and peel off cleanly when warmed gently with a hairdryer. Window clings use static, not glue, so they leave nothing behind. Test any adhesive in a hidden spot first.

When should I put up Hanukkah decorations in 2026?

Hanukkah 2026 begins on the evening of Monday 14 December and ends on the evening of Tuesday 22 December. Most people decorate in the first week of December, so the display is ready well before the first night. Put up the longer-lasting pieces — the garland, the window clings, the wall decals and the pillow covers — in early December, and save the table dressing and the gelt jars for the day before the festival starts, so everything feels fresh on night one.

What is the cheapest way to decorate for Hanukkah in a rental?

Start with the three pieces that change the room most for the least money: a set of window clings, a battery fairy light string and a dreidel scatter for the table. Together they cost around $25 and make the whole flat feel festive. Add a flameless menorah as the centrepiece when your budget allows, and reuse everything next year — window clings, garlands, pillow covers and decals all pack away flat and survive several festivals.

Will window clings and wall decals really come off without a mark?

Yes, if you buy the right kind. Window clings stick with static and peel off clean, leaving nothing on the glass. Removable vinyl wall decals are designed to come off without lifting paint, but the key word is removable — permanent vinyl will damage a painted wall. Test one decal in a hidden spot, wait a day, and peel it off before doing the whole wall. On freshly painted walls, wait at least a month after painting before applying any decal.
