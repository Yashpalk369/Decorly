9 Renter-Friendly Christmas Tree Alternatives Under $25

Rental-Friendly Decor November 11, 2026·2,139 words

Nine renter-friendly Christmas tree alternatives under $25 — wall trees, ladder trees, potted trees and branch displays, no real tree needed.

Not every renter wants a Christmas tree, and not every small flat has room for one. Some landlords ban real trees outright. Some flats have no corner free. Some tenants have allergies, or cats that climb, or a deposit they would rather not risk on a toppled tree and a carpet full of pine sap. Whatever the reason, skipping the tree does not mean skipping Christmas — it just means the tree takes a different shape.

Every alternative in this roundup costs $25 or less, needs no drilling, no nails and no permanent fixings, and packs flat or fits in a cupboard in January. They range from a felt tree stuck to the wall with removable hooks to a potted rosemary that you cook with afterwards. Pick the one that suits your space, your household and your tolerance for pine needles, and hang your baubles on something that was designed for a small rented room.

1. Felt Wall Tree with Ornaments

A felt wall tree is a flat tree shape made of green felt, stuck to the wall with removable adhesive dots, with felt ornaments that press on and peel off. It costs $12–20, takes up no floor space at all, and is the best tree alternative for households with small children or cats, because there is nothing to knock over and nothing breakable at toddler height. The ornaments can be rearranged daily, which children love, and the whole tree peels off the wall in January leaving nothing behind.

Why it works: It gives you the silhouette of a tree, the fun of decorating it, and zero floor space, zero needles and zero risk to your deposit.

Where it fits: On a living room wall at child height, or on the inside of a bedroom door in a flat with no spare wall.

2. Wooden Ladder Tree

A small wooden ladder, leaned against the wall and dressed with fairy lights and ornaments, is the most stylish tree alternative on this list. A decorative ladder costs $18–25, or use a step ladder you already own, and the rungs become shelves for ornaments, small gifts and candles. It takes up less floor space than a real tree, leans rather than stands, so it cannot topple, and works as a blanket ladder for the other eleven months of the year.

Why it works: It looks intentional rather than like a compromise, and it earns its keep all year as storage and display.

Where it fits: Against a living room wall in a narrow gap a real tree could never fit into, or in a hallway that needs a festive touch.

3. Potted Rosemary Tree

A potted rosemary plant trained into a cone shape is a living Christmas tree that fits on a sideboard. It costs $10–20 from a garden centre, smells wonderful, and after Christmas you keep cooking with it — it is the only tree alternative that ends up in your roast potatoes. Decorate it with miniature baubles and one short string of battery fairy lights, keeping everything tiny and light so the delicate branches do not snap. Why it works: It is a real, living plant that smells like Christmas, takes up the space of a houseplant, and keeps giving after the season ends.

Where it fits: On a bright windowsill or sideboard in a small flat, where it doubles as a kitchen herb all year.

4. Bare Branch in a Vase

A tall bare branch in a vase, hung with ornaments, is the minimalist's tree: sculptural, elegant, and almost free. A branch from a park or garden, a tall vase you already own, and a dozen ornaments you already have are the only ingredients, and the whole thing costs under $10 if you buy the ornaments new. It suits a modern flat far better than a plastic tree, it takes up the footprint of a single vase, and it looks just as good bare in January with a few fairy lights as it does dressed in December.

Why it works: It turns ornaments into the decoration and the branch into the display, so the focus is on the things you love rather than on a tree shape.

Where it fits: On a sideboard, a dining table or a hallway console, anywhere a tall vase already lives.

5. Mini Tabletop Tree

A mini tabletop tree, 30 to 60 centimetres tall, is the closest thing to a real tree without the floor space. Artificial mini trees cost $10–22, come pre-lit or plain, and sit on a side table, a shelf or a windowsill where a full tree could never go. Choose one with a sturdy base so it cannot be knocked over, and keep the ornaments small — full-size baubles overwhelm a mini tree and drag the branches down. It stores in a shoebox in January, which is the entire point.

Why it works: It is a real tree shape at a fraction of the size, so it satisfies the urge for a tree without demanding a corner of the room.

Where it fits: On a side table next to the sofa, on a bedroom dresser, or on a desk for a festive workspace.

6. Wall Decal Tree

A wall decal tree is a large vinyl sticker in the shape of a tree, applied to a painted wall and peeled off in January. Decal trees cost $12–20, come in dozens of designs from simple green silhouettes to trees made of lights or words, and are the fastest tree alternative to install — ten minutes, no tools, no mess. They are designed to peel off cleanly, but always test one corner in a hidden spot first, and never apply them to fresh paint or wallpaper.

Why it works: It gives you a full-size tree on a wall you could never put a real tree against, and it disappears without a trace.

Where it fits: On the largest blank wall in the living room, or in a child's bedroom where a real tree would be a hazard.

7. Bookshelf Tree

A bookshelf tree uses books you already own, arranged in a triangle on a shelf to suggest a tree shape, with fairy lights and a few ornaments tucked between the spines. It costs nothing, it uses furniture that is already in the room, and it is the most personal tree alternative on this list — the books are yours, the arrangement is yours, and no two bookshelf trees look alike. Stack the books in decreasing widths, green and red spines towards the front if you have them, and finish with a star or a bow on top.

Why it works: It turns a piece of furniture you already have into a Christmas display, with no purchase and no storage needed.

Where it fits: On any bookshelf in the flat, especially a small one in a bedroom or hallway that would otherwise go undecorated.

8. Tomato Cage Tree

A tomato cage — the wire cone used to support tomato plants — wrapped in greenery and fairy lights makes a surprisingly convincing tree. The cage costs $8–15 from a garden centre, the greenery can be fresh branches or a cheap garland, and the cone shape is already a tree shape, so the structure does the work for you. It is light, it folds flat, and it is the best tree alternative for a balcony or a small garden, where a real tree would be battered by the wind.

Why it works: It is a proper three-dimensional tree shape for the price of a garden accessory, and it stores flat against a wall.

Where it fits: In a corner of the living room, on a balcony, or in a hallway where a real tree would block the walkway.

9. Hanging Ornament Tree

A hanging ornament tree is a cluster of ornaments suspended from a single ceiling hook or a curtain rod at different heights, so that from across the room they form the shape of a tree floating in mid-air. It costs nothing if you already own ornaments — just fishing line or clear thread and a removable ceiling hook — and it is the most dramatic tree alternative on this list. It also takes up no floor space whatsoever, which is the point: the tree is in the air, and the floor underneath is free.

Why it works: It is a showstopper that uses vertical space a real tree can never use, and it costs almost nothing.

Where it fits: Hanging from a ceiling hook in a living room corner, or from a curtain rod in front of a window where the light catches the ornaments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best Christmas tree alternative for a small rental?

A felt wall tree or a mini tabletop tree. The felt tree takes no floor space at all and is safe for children and pets, while a mini tree gives you a real tree shape on a side table. Both cost under $25 and leave no mark on the flat.

Can I have a Christmas tree if my landlord bans real trees?

Yes. Most landlord bans cover real trees because of needles, sap and fire risk. Artificial alternatives like a felt wall tree, a ladder tree or a decal tree do not shed, do not drip sap, and are usually fine — but check your tenancy agreement first, as some bans cover all trees.

How do I hang ornaments without damaging the walls?

Use removable adhesive hooks for wall displays, fishing line from a ceiling hook for hanging trees, and existing furniture like shelves, ladders and curtain rods wherever possible. Never use nails or screws, and test any adhesive in a hidden spot first.

Are tree alternatives cheaper than a real tree?

Usually, yes. A real tree costs £30–60 every year, plus a stand and disposal. Most alternatives on this list cost under $25 once and last for years, and several — the branch, the bookshelf tree and the hanging ornament tree — cost nothing at all.

What do I do with a tree alternative in January?

Most of them pack flat: the felt tree folds, the decal peels off, the ladder becomes a blanket ladder, the tomato cage folds against a wall, and the mini tree fits in a shoebox. The rosemary tree stays on the windowsill and goes into your cooking.
