9 Halloween Decor Finds Under $15 for Small Apartments

Small Space Decor October 18, 2026·2,013 words

9 Halloween decor finds under $15 for small apartments — LED pumpkins, bat decals, spiderweb throws — all damage-free and renter-friendly.

Halloween is thirteen days away, which means the good cheap stuff is about to sell out. The trick to decorating a small apartment for Halloween isn't buying more — it's buying small, buying removable, and buying things that earn their square footage. Every find on this list costs under $15, leaves zero damage (your deposit is safe), and works in a studio or one-bedroom where a six-foot skeleton simply isn't happening.

1. Flameless LED pumpkin lanterns (set of 3)

Real pumpkins rot; LED pumpkin lanterns glow all month. A set of three flameless pumpkins ($10–14) with built-in timers turns a windowsill or entryway console into an instant Halloween display — and they run on batteries, so there are no cords to hide in a small space.

Why it works: pumpkins are the one Halloween symbol that reads festive rather than tacky, and the warm LED glow doubles as evening mood lighting. The timer means they light themselves at dusk — set and forget.

Where it fits: windowsill (visible from the street, which counts double), entryway console, or a bathroom counter for a surprise guests won't expect. Shop LED pumpkin lanterns

2. Removable 3D bat wall decals (set of 24)

A sheet of 3D bat decals ($6–9) is the highest-impact-per-dollar Halloween buy that exists. Stick them in a swooping flock across one wall, above a doorway, or trailing up a staircase wall — they peel off clean on November 1st with zero residue.

Why it works: bats add vertical drama to walls without taking a single centimetre of floor space, which is everything in a small apartment. The 3D folded wings catch light and cast tiny shadows, so they look far more expensive than a sticker sheet has any right to.

Where it fits: one living room wall, a hallway, or inside a large mirror frame. Start at a corner and let the flock "fly" outward — random clusters look natural, straight lines look like a classroom. Shop 3D bat wall decals

3. Stretchable spiderweb with spiders

A bag of stretchable spiderweb ($5–8) covers an entire corner, bookshelf, or doorway — and it stretches absurdly far, so one bag decorates a whole studio. The included plastic spiders sell the effect; scatter a few, don't dump the whole bag.

Why it works: spiderweb turns dead corners (the top of a bookshelf, the space above a door) into decorated space without moving a single piece of furniture. In a small apartment, decorating the corners you normally ignore makes the whole place feel transformed.

Where it fits: stretch across a bookshelf top, drape over a floor lamp, or web up one corner of the living room. A few strands on a houseplant also works — instant haunted conservatory. Shop stretchable spiderweb

4. Halloween throw pillow covers (set of 2)

Covers, not pillows: a set of two Halloween pillow covers ($9–13) swaps onto the inserts you already own in thirty seconds. Pumpkins, ghosts, or black cats on cream or orange — pick one design, not three, or the sofa looks like a costume shop.

Why it works: textiles are the fastest room refresh, and covers store flat in a drawer for eleven months. One patterned pair against your everyday solids is the difference between "decorated" and "overwhelmed" in a small living room.

Where it fits: sofa, bed, or reading chair. Pair with a solid black or burnt-orange throw you already own — no need to buy the matching set. Shop halloween pillow covers

5. Battery-operated orange string lights

A 20-foot string of orange LED fairy lights ($7–11) is Halloween's answer to Christmas lights — drape them over a bookshelf, around a window frame, or through your houseplants and the whole room glows autumn. Battery operation means no outlet hunting behind rental furniture.

Why it works: colored light changes a room's entire mood for the price of a pizza. Orange is warm enough to live with all month (unlike purple or green, which get old fast), and the lights transition straight into Thanksgiving.

Where it fits: bookshelf edges, curtain rods, headboards, or woven through a large plant. Use the built-in timer so they glow at dusk automatically. Shop orange fairy lights

6. Window cling ghosts and pumpkins

Static-cling window decorations ($5–8 for a sheet) stick with water, peel off with zero residue, and are visible from both sides — which means your Halloween decor advertises to the street while you enjoy it from the sofa. Ghosts, pumpkins, and "trick or treat" lettering all come in one pack.

Why it works: windows are free display space in a small apartment, and clings use every centimetre of it. They're also the only Halloween decor that's genuinely fun for kids passing by — instant neighbourhood goodwill.

Where it fits: living room window, glass balcony door, or a bathroom mirror for a jump-scare guests will actually laugh at. Dampen the glass first and smooth out bubbles for a clean look. Shop halloween window clings

7. Black cat figurine or plush

One small black cat — a $8–12 ceramic figurine or a plush — perched on a bookshelf or side table is the kind of single statement piece that anchors a whole Halloween shelf. It's also the one item on this list you'll happily leave out year-round if the style fits.

Why it works: a single sculptural object reads as decor, not decoration. In a small apartment, one confident piece beats ten scattered trinkets — it gives the eye somewhere to land instead of visual clutter everywhere.

Where it fits: bookshelf styling (nestle it between books), a side table vignette with a candle, or the entryway console greeting guests. Matte black finishes look expensive; glossy ones look like party-store stock. Shop black cat figurines

8. Halloween door banner or hanging sign

A hanging door sign ($7–10) on a removable adhesive hook is the renter's wreath — see our full Halloween rental decor guide for more door-and-entry ideas. — "Happy Halloween" lettering, a witch-hat design, or a spooky welcome message that hangs on the inside or outside of your door. No nails, no damage, full curb appeal.

Why it works: the door is the first thing guests (and trick-or-treaters) see, and it's the easiest surface to decorate in a rental. One sign does the work of an entire porch display, which you don't have in an apartment anyway.

Where it fits: front door (inside for hallway buildings, outside for ground-floor flats), or hung on a bedroom door for kids. A Command-style hook holds it all month and pops off clean. Shop halloween door signs

9. Skull or pumpkin candy bowl

A skull-shaped or pumpkin-shaped candy bowl ($9–14) is Halloween decor that does a job: it holds the trick-or-treat candy, sits on the console as a sculptural piece, and gets reused every October. Fill it early and it's decor; fill it on the 31st and it's functional.

Why it works: dual-purpose pieces are a small-apartment survival strategy. This bowl earns its shelf space eleven months as storage (keys, odds and ends) and one month as the star of your Halloween display.

Where it fits: entryway console, coffee table, or kitchen counter. Ceramic skulls look convincingly gothic; plastic pumpkins read friendlier for households with small kids. Shop skull candy bowls

How to style them together in a small space

Nine pieces could crowd a studio, so use the 3-3-3 formula: three wall/vertical pieces (bat decals, spiderweb, door sign), three light/textile pieces (LED pumpkins, orange lights, pillow covers), and three tabletop pieces (black cat, candy bowl, window clings don't count — they're free space). That's a fully decorated apartment from one shopping trip under $100 total.

Keep everything removable and everything under $15, and when November arrives the whole lot packs into a single storage box. That's the renter's Halloween: maximum atmosphere, zero deposit risk, and a box that fits on top of the wardrobe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I decorate for Halloween in a rental without losing my deposit?

Yes — every item on this list is damage-free: static clings, removable decals, battery lights, and pieces that sit on surfaces. Avoid anything that needs nails, screws, or adhesive that isn't explicitly removable. When in doubt, use a removable hook and test decals on an inconspicuous spot first.

How early should I buy Halloween decor?

Now — mid-October. The under-$15 sweet spot sells out first, and prices climb in the final week. Buying early also means you actually enjoy the decorations for the full season instead of panic-buying on October 30th.

How do I decorate a studio apartment for Halloween without clutter?

Decorate vertically and use surfaces you already have. Wall decals, window clings, and string lights add atmosphere without taking floor space. Limit tabletop pieces to one or two per surface, and pick a tight color palette (orange + black + cream) so everything reads as coordinated rather than chaotic.

What's the cheapest way to make an apartment feel Halloweeny?

Light and one statement wall. A $7 string of orange lights plus a $6 sheet of bat decals transforms a room for $13 total. Everything else is bonus. Dim your regular lamps, let the orange glow do the work, and guests will swear you went all out.

Can I reuse any of this for Thanksgiving or Christmas?

The orange string lights and LED candles transition straight into Thanksgiving — our hosting guide shows how to carry the look through November. The black cat figurine and candy bowl work year-round as regular decor. Pillow covers, bat decals, and window clings are Halloween-only — but they all store flat in a single box.

Are LED pumpkins safe around kids and pets?

Much safer than real candles in carved pumpkins — no flame, no heat, no melted wax. Battery compartments should be screwed shut (check before buying if you have toddlers who pry things open). They're also lightweight, so a knocked-over pumpkin is a non-event rather than a smashed ceramic disaster.
