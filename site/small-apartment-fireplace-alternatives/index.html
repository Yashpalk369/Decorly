10 Fireplace Alternatives for Small Apartments That Still Feel Like Christmas

Small Space Decor October 28, 2026·2,438 words

10 fireplace alternatives for small apartments — electric stoves, candle clusters, faux mantels and more that bring the firelight feeling home.

Nothing says Christmas quite like a fireplace, and nothing says small apartment quite like not having one. No chimney, no mantel, no landlord permission for anything involving actual fire — and yet the feeling a fireplace gives is not really about the fire. It is about a warm focal point, flickering light, and a place to hang the stockings. Every idea below recreates that feeling without a single structural change, and most of them cost less than a nice dinner out.

The trick is to decide which part of the fireplace you actually miss. If it is the glow, candles and LED flames will do it. If it is the mantel moment, a freestanding shelf or console gives you somewhere to style garland and stockings. If it is the heat, a small electric stove genuinely warms a room. Match the alternative to the feeling and you will stop noticing the missing chimney altogether.

1. A small electric fireplace stove

Why it works: an electric stove is the closest thing to a real fireplace that a renter can own. It plugs into a normal socket, produces real heat with a thermostat, and the flame effect is convincing from sofa distance — especially the newer models with glowing logs and adjustable flame brightness. It also solves the focal-point problem instantly: a stove in a corner anchors the whole room the way a fireplace anchors a big house.

Where it fits: in an empty corner or against the wall where a fireplace would logically go, ideally with a small rug in front of it to define the "hearth" zone. Choose a compact model (under 45 cm wide) so it does not eat floor space, and skip anything with a fake stone surround — the simple cast-iron-look stoves read far more expensive. In summer it becomes a side table with a plant on top. Shop small electric fireplace stoves

2. A freestanding faux mantel shelf

Why it works: half the fireplace fantasy is the mantel — the shelf where the garland goes, the stockings hang and the candles sit. A freestanding mantel surround leans against the wall, needs no fixings, and gives you all of that styling surface without touching the wall at all. When Christmas ends, it stays up as a display shelf for plants and books.

Where it fits: flat against any blank wall, ideally the one your sofa faces. Style it like a real mantel: garland draped across the top, two candle clusters at either end, stockings on removable hooks. The firebox opening is the perfect spot for a basket of logs, a cluster of pillar candles or a stack of wrapped presents. Measure your wall first — mantels come in widths from 90 cm to 130 cm, and one that is too wide for the wall looks like furniture that wandered in from another house. 3. A cluster of pillar candles on a tray

Why it works: real flame, real flicker, zero installation — a tray of pillar candles at different heights is the oldest fireplace substitute there is, and it works because firelight is firelight. Grouped tightly, the flames merge into one warm glow that reads as a hearth from across the room, and the tray keeps wax contained and the whole arrangement portable.

Where it fits: on the floor against a wall, on a low console, or inside an existing alcove or unused nook. Use flameless LED pillars if your lease bans real candles or you have pets — modern ones flicker convincingly and run on a timer, so the "fire" lights itself every evening at six. Either way, vary the heights (at least three) and keep the tray itself simple: wood, brass or stone, nothing patterned enough to compete with the glow. Shop pillar candles in mixed heights

4. A fireplace loop on the television

Why it works: it sounds like a joke until you try it on a December evening with the lights low. A high-quality fireplace video — crackling logs, real flames, ambient sound — turns the biggest screen in the room into a hearth, and it costs nothing if you already own the TV. Paired with a couple of real candles for the scent and warmth, the effect is surprisingly convincing.

Where it fits: wherever the TV already lives, ideally with the sofa arranged to face it as you would a fireplace. Search for a 4K fireplace loop with sound, dim the overhead lights, and add a bowl of pinecones or a garland on the TV stand to sell the illusion. This is also the best option for the tiniest studios: no floor space, no storage, no purchase — just an evening's atmosphere on demand. Shop LED TV backlight strips for a warm glow

5. Lanterns with LED flame candles

Why it works: lanterns give firelight a shape. A cluster of black metal or wooden lanterns with flickering LED candles inside reads as intentional decor rather than a workaround, and they work in every room — hallway, bedroom, bathroom — so the fireplace feeling follows you around the flat instead of living in one corner.

Where it fits: in a group of three on the floor beside the sofa, on a windowsill, or flanking the front door. Mix sizes (one tall, one medium, one small) and keep them all the same finish for a collected look. Battery LED candles with timers mean they light themselves every evening and never need attention, which is exactly the kind of low-effort magic a fireplace is supposed to be. 6. A DIY cardboard fireplace

Why it works: the cardboard fireplace is a Christmas classic for a reason — it costs almost nothing, it folds flat for storage, and children and adults alike love it far more than they expect to. A few large boxes, some white paint or brick-effect wrapping paper, and you have a full mantel, firebox and hearth that looks genuinely charming in photos and in person.

Where it fits: against the main living-room wall, styled exactly like a real mantel. Keep the proportions modest — a fireplace wider than your sofa looks like a stage set — and fill the firebox with a candle cluster or a string of warm fairy lights behind a few paper logs. The secret to making it look good rather than crafty is restraint: one colour (white or cream), clean edges, and no glitter. When January comes, it flattens into a single box behind the wardrobe. 7. A tabletop ethanol fireplace

Why it works: a tabletop ethanol burner is a real, living flame in a box the size of a bread loaf. It needs no chimney, no gas line and no installation — just ethanol fuel — and it puts actual dancing firelight on the coffee table. For apartments where a full electric stove feels too big, this is the fireplace that fits on furniture you already own.

Where it fits: on a coffee table, a sideboard or a sturdy windowsill, well away from curtains and always on a heatproof surface. Check your lease first — some buildings restrict open flames even in this form — and never leave it burning unattended. The flame is small and the heat output is modest, so think of it as atmosphere rather than heating, and pair it with a chunky throw and the good mugs for the full effect. 8. A basket of logs with fairy lights

Why it works: sometimes the fireplace you want is really just the suggestion of one — the wood, the warmth, the glow. A basket of birch logs wrapped in warm-white fairy lights gives you all three for the price of a string of lights, and it doubles as genuinely pretty decor long after Christmas, when the lights come out and the logs stay.

Where it fits: beside the sofa, under a console table, or in any empty corner that needs a focal point. Choose real birch logs (the white bark is the whole look) rather than fake ones, and wind the lights through the logs rather than draping them on top — the light should come from inside the pile, like embers. Battery fairy lights with a timer keep it effortless. Shop birch logs and warm fairy lights

9. A peel-and-stick fireplace wall mural

Why it works: a large peel-and-stick fireplace decal turns a blank wall into a fireplace in about ten minutes, peels off cleanly in January, and costs less than a takeaway. Modern decals are printed with realistic brick, mantel and even flame detail, and from sofa distance they genuinely read as a fireplace — especially once you hang stockings on removable hooks "from the mantel" and set candles in front.

Where it fits: on the main wall of the living room, centred like a real fireplace would be. Measure carefully and hang it low — the mantel line should sit at roughly waist height, not eye level, or the illusion breaks. This pairs brilliantly with a candle cluster on the floor in front of it: the decal provides the architecture, the candles provide the light, and together they make a hearth. Shop peel-and-stick fireplace wall decals

10. The no-fire fireplace ritual

Why it works: here is the honest truth behind this whole list — the fireplace feeling is mostly a ritual. A specific chair, a chunky throw, candlelight, a hot drink, and the decision that this corner is where Christmas evenings happen. Build the ritual and the room feels like it has a fireplace whether or not anything flickers, because the feeling was never really about the fire.

Where it fits: any corner with a comfortable seat and a small side table. The ingredients are simple: a thick knit throw, a candle or two, a mug you love, and a rule — screens off, lights low, one hour of Christmas music or a book. Add a garland on the nearest shelf and stockings on the nearest hook, and guests will describe your flat as "so cozy" without ever noticing there is no fireplace to point at.
