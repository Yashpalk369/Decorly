9 Winter Greenery Wreaths Under $25 for Small Apartments

Plants & Greenery November 12, 2026·1,950 words

Nine winter greenery wreaths under $25 for small apartments — evergreen, eucalyptus, pinecone and berry wreaths that dress a door without eating your budget.

A wreath is the fastest way to make a small apartment feel like winter has arrived, and it is one of the cheapest decorations you can buy. A good greenery wreath hangs on the inside of a front door, a bedroom door or a blank wall, needs no floor space, and lasts from the first week of December until New Year — or all winter, if you choose one without Christmas-specific details. Every wreath in this roundup costs $25 or less, and each one was chosen for a small space first: light enough for a removable adhesive hook, small enough for a flat door, and simple enough to suit a rented home.

Measure your door before you buy: a wreath of 30 to 45 centimetres suits a standard flat door, while anything larger overwhelms it and anything smaller disappears. Hang it on the inside of the front door if your landlord controls the outside, or on a bedroom door or a living-room wall if the front door is not an option. Pick one that matches your room, hang it in thirty seconds, and the flat is dressed.

1. Classic Fresh Evergreen Wreath

A fresh evergreen wreath is the original, and it is still the best: real pine, fir or cedar branches, a pinecone or two, and the smell of a forest every time you open the door. Fresh wreaths cost $15–25 from a florist or a garden centre, last three to four weeks if you mist them every couple of days, and compost cleanly in January. Nothing artificial smells like a real evergreen wreath, and nothing photographs like one either.

Why it works: Real greenery brings scent as well as colour, and the natural variation in the branches makes every wreath look handmade.

Where it fits: The inside of a front door or a living-room wall in a flat that wants a traditional Christmas feel without a tree.

2. Dried Eucalyptus Wreath

A dried eucalyptus wreath is the wreath for people who want greenery without the Christmas clichés. Soft silver-green leaves on a simple ring, no baubles, no ribbon, just the gentle scent of eucalyptus and a shape that suits a modern flat all winter long. Dried eucalyptus wreaths cost $18–25, last for months without any care, and fade gracefully rather than dropping needles on the floor.

Why it works: It is understated, long-lasting and season-neutral, so it works from November to March without looking like a leftover decoration.

Where it fits: A bedroom door, a bathroom wall or a hallway in a flat with a calm, modern palette and a love of natural textures.

3. Pinecone and Evergreen Wreath

Pinecones are the cheapest wreath material there is, which is why a pinecone and evergreen wreath costs $12–20 and looks far more expensive. The cones add texture and a woodland feel that plain greenery cannot, they catch the light beautifully, and they last all winter without dropping or fading. Look for a wreath where the cones are wired on securely rather than glued, because glued cones fall off the first time the door slams.

Why it works: Pinecones add weight, texture and a natural woodland character that makes a simple evergreen wreath look crafted rather than bought.

Where it fits: A rustic or farmhouse-style flat, hung on a front door or above a fireplace alternative, paired with candles and warm wood.

4. Dried Orange and Cinnamon Wreath

Dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks turn a plain greenery wreath into something that smells like Christmas baking. The orange slices add warm amber colour against the green, the cinnamon adds a spicy scent, and the whole wreath costs $15–22 ready-made — or under $10 if you dry your own orange slices in a low oven and wire them onto a plain evergreen wreath. It is the most fragrant wreath on this list, and the scent lasts for weeks.

Why it works: It adds colour and scent in one move, and the warm orange against green is the most festive colour combination there is.

Where it fits: A kitchen door or a dining-room wall, where the scent of orange and cinnamon belongs with the cooking.

5. Frosted Artificial Wreath

A frosted artificial wreath is the practical choice: it looks like a snowy evergreen, it never drops needles, and it packs flat into a box in January to come out again next year. Frosted wreaths cost $18–25, last for years, and suit flats where a real wreath would dry out too fast — a warm flat, a door near a radiator, or a household with allergies. Shake it outside before hanging to lose the loose flocking, and it will not shed indoors.

Why it works: It gives you the snowy winter look with zero maintenance, and one purchase covers every Christmas for years.

Where it fits: A front door in a warm flat, a hallway wall, or any spot where a fresh wreath would dry out in a week.

6. Magnolia Leaf Wreath

A magnolia leaf wreath is the most elegant wreath on this list, and the one most likely to stay up after Christmas. Glossy deep-green leaves with warm brown undersides, arranged in overlapping layers, give a rich, sculptural look that suits a modern or neo-deco flat far better than traditional pine. Magnolia wreaths cost $20–25, last all winter, and work as well in February as they do in December.

Why it works: The glossy leaves catch the light and add a luxe, botanical feel without any glitter or baubles.

Where it fits: A living-room wall, a bedroom door or a hallway in a flat with a sophisticated palette — deep green, brass and cream.

7. Red Berry Wreath

Red berries are the colour pop that a plain greenery wreath needs, and a berry wreath delivers it for $15–22. Clusters of bright red berries — real or artificial — against dark green foliage read as festive from across the room, and they work with every style from traditional to modern. Artificial berries last for years and never stain, while real berries last a few weeks and need to be kept away from pets and small children.

Why it works: The red berries give a plain green wreath a focal point and a festive colour accent without any extra decoration.

Where it fits: A front door that needs to be seen from the hallway, or a living-room wall where the wreath is the only red in the room.

8. Minimalist Greenery Hoop Wreath

A minimalist hoop wreath — a thin gold or black metal ring with a small cluster of greenery at the bottom — is the wreath for small flats that cannot take a full, heavy wreath. It weighs almost nothing, hangs on a single small adhesive hook, and leaves most of the wall visible, which makes a small room feel larger rather than smaller. Hoop wreaths cost $12–20 and suit a modern, Scandinavian or minimalist flat perfectly.

Why it works: It gives you the greenery and the festive gesture without the bulk, the weight or the needle drop of a full wreath.

Where it fits: A small bedroom door, a narrow hallway wall, or above a desk in a flat where space is the scarcest resource.

Shop minimalist hoop wreaths

9. Olive Branch Wreath

An olive branch wreath is the quietest wreath on this list, and the one that lasts longest. Soft grey-green olive leaves on a simple ring, with no berries, no cones and no glitter, give a calm Mediterranean feel that works all winter and well into spring. Olive wreaths cost $18–25, artificial versions last for years, and the muted colour suits flats with a sage, cream and terracotta palette.

Why it works: It is subtle, long-lasting and season-neutral, so it never looks like a Christmas decoration that overstayed its welcome.

Where it fits: A kitchen wall, a bedroom door or a hallway in a flat with a warm, Mediterranean or bohemian style.

Frequently Asked Questions

What size wreath suits a small apartment door?

A wreath of 30 to 45 centimetres suits a standard flat door. Anything larger overwhelms the door and the room, while anything smaller disappears. Measure the door before you buy, and remember that a wreath hangs best with at least 10 centimetres of clear door around it.

How do I hang a wreath in a rental without damage?

Use a removable adhesive hook rated for the wreath's weight, or hang it from a door hanger that hooks over the top of the door. Never nail into a rented door or use tape on painted surfaces. Light wreaths under a kilogram work fine on a single medium adhesive hook.

How long does a fresh evergreen wreath last indoors?

Three to four weeks if you mist it every couple of days and keep it away from radiators and direct heat. A wreath hung on a cool hallway wall lasts longer than one hung near a heat source. When the needles start to drop heavily, compost it and replace it with a dried or artificial wreath for the rest of winter.

Can I leave a greenery wreath up after Christmas?

Yes, if you choose a season-neutral wreath — eucalyptus, magnolia, olive or a minimalist hoop — it looks right all winter. Wreaths with baubles, glitter or explicitly Christmas details should come down in January, but plain greenery works from November to March.
