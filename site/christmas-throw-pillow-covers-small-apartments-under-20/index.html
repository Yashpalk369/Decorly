9 Christmas Throw Pillow Covers Under $20 for Small Apartments

Small Space Decor October 29, 2026·1,895 words

9 Christmas throw pillow covers under $20 for small apartments — plaid, velvet, knit and more that swap in over your everyday inserts for instant holiday cheer.

Throw pillow covers are the single best-value Christmas decoration for a small apartment, and it is not close. A cover costs a few pounds, folds flat into a drawer in January, and transforms a sofa you already own — no storage unit, no loft, no new furniture. Swap the covers in late November, swap them back in January, and the whole room reads as Christmas without a single other purchase. This roundup picks nine covers under $20 that actually look good in a small space, where one wrong pattern can overwhelm the whole room.

The rule for small rooms is scale: pick one or two statement covers and let the rest be quiet. A big buffalo plaid or a bold reindeer print works as a single accent; four of them on a two-seater sofa looks like a gift shop exploded. Buy covers, not full pillows — your existing inserts do the work, and covers store in a fraction of the space. All prices below are typical for the cover style; check the listing for the size, since a cover that does not fit your insert will look worse than no cover at all.

1. The classic red buffalo plaid

Why it works: buffalo plaid is the Christmas pattern that never dates, and in a small apartment its bold checks do a lot of decorating work from a single cushion. Red and black reads instantly festive against a neutral sofa, and the large-scale check suits the chunky, cozy textures of winter — wool throws, knit blankets, warm lighting.

Where it fits: as the one statement cushion on a small sofa or armchair, paired with plain cream or charcoal covers. One plaid is a statement; two is a theme; three is too many in a small room. Shop red buffalo plaid pillow covers

2. The soft velvet solid in forest green

Why it works: velvet adds instant luxury for very little money, and forest green is festive without shouting about it. A solid velvet cover is the easiest way to make a budget sofa look expensive at Christmas, and it works with every other pattern in this roundup — plaid, knit, or embroidered — because it gives the eye somewhere quiet to rest.

Where it fits: on any sofa, but especially in rooms where the Christmas tree or garland already provides the pattern. Pair a green velvet with a cream knit throw and the room feels considered rather than decorated-by-accident. Shop forest green velvet pillow covers

3. The chunky knit cable cover

Why it works: a chunky cable knit cover looks and feels like a jumper for your sofa, which is exactly the cozy winter mood most small apartments are chasing in December. Cream or oatmeal knit is neutral enough to stay out from Christmas to February, so it earns a longer season than a purely festive print — a real advantage when storage is tight.

Where it fits: beside a plaid or a green velvet, on a reading chair, or on a bed as a winter accent that outlasts the tree. Knit covers hide wear well, which matters on a sofa that gets daily use. Shop chunky knit pillow covers

4. The embroidered pine branch

Why it works: embroidered pine branches give you Christmas greenery that never drops needles and never needs watering. The design is subtle from across the room and detailed up close, which is the right balance for a small space — festive without dominating — and it suits rooms that lean minimal or Scandinavian rather than traditional.

Where it fits: on a light-coloured sofa where the embroidery shows, or as a pair flanking a plain centre cushion. This is the cover to buy if you want guests to notice the detail rather than the theme. Shop embroidered pine branch pillow covers

5. The tartan with a modern twist

Why it works: tartan is plaid's more grown-up cousin, and the muted, modern tartans — softer reds, dusty greens, a thread of gold — read festive without reading costume-y. In a small apartment, a muted tartan is easier to live with for six weeks than a loud buffalo check, and it pairs with the velvet and knit covers in this list without clashing.

Where it fits: on a grey or beige sofa, where the muted colours warm the room without fighting it. One tartan cushion plus one velvet cushion is a complete Christmas sofa scheme for a two-seater. Shop muted tartan pillow covers

6. The "Merry Christmas" typographic cover

Why it works: a simple typographic cover — "Merry Christmas" in a clean serif or a friendly script — is the most direct way to say Christmas on a sofa, and it works precisely because it is so simple. No pattern to clash with, no colours to match beyond red and cream, and it photographs beautifully for anyone whose sofa ends up in Christmas photos.

Where it fits: as the single festive cushion in a room that is otherwise neutral, or on a bench in an entryway where guests see it first. Keep it to one per room; two typographic cushions start to feel like signage. Shop Merry Christmas typographic pillow covers

7. The faux fur winter white

Why it works: faux fur is the texture equivalent of turning the heating up — it makes a room feel warmer before anyone sits down. Winter white faux fur reads as snow rather than Christmas specifically, so it works from the first cold snap through January, and it softens the harder patterns like plaid and tartan when they share a sofa.

Where it fits: on a dark sofa where white fur pops, or in a bedroom as a winter accent. Faux fur sheds a little at first, so give it a shake outside before it goes on the sofa, and keep it away from the fireplace or candles. Shop white faux fur pillow covers

8. The reindeer silhouette in linen

Why it works: a single reindeer silhouette on natural linen is festive, graphic, and calm all at once — the kind of cover that suits a renter who likes Christmas but not clutter. Linen's texture keeps it from looking like a novelty print, and the neutral base means it works with almost any sofa colour.

Where it fits: as a pair on a three-seater, or a single on an armchair beside the tree. This is the safest festive cover to buy as a gift, because its taste is hard to argue with. Shop reindeer linen pillow covers

9. The set of four mix-and-match covers

Why it works: a coordinated set of four — usually a plaid, a solid, a typographic, and a pattern — takes all the guesswork out of matching, and per-cover it is almost always the cheapest way to dress a sofa for Christmas. For a small apartment, a set is also the easiest to store: four flat covers in one zip bag, ready for next year.

Where it fits: on a larger sofa or across a sofa and two chairs, where a set can be spread out rather than crammed together. Check the set's sizes before buying — a set of 18-inch covers on 20-inch inserts will look under-stuffed. Shop Christmas pillow cover sets of 4
