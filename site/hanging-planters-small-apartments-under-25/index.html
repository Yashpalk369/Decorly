9 Hanging Planters Under $25 for Small Apartments

Plants & Greenery October 30, 2026·2,071 words

9 hanging planters under $25 for small apartments — macrame, ceramic, self-watering and no-drill picks that add plants without using a single inch of floor.

Hanging planters are the small apartment's secret weapon. They put plants where nothing else can go — in the air — which means you can own ten plants in a flat with no floor space for even one more pot. They draw the eye up, they make ceilings feel higher, and they keep plants away from pets, toddlers, and the vacuum cleaner. The catch is that hanging planters range from $5 string hangers to $80 ceramic showpieces, and not all of them suit a rental, where ceiling hooks may be forbidden and a falling pot is a deposit deduction.

Every pick below is under $25, and every one was chosen for rental life: light enough for adhesive or tension hanging, kind to plants with drainage or a liner, and easy to take down when you move. Before you buy, check two things: what your ceiling or wall can hold, and how you will water the plant without dripping on the floor below.

1. The classic cotton macrame hanger, set of 2

Why it works: macrame is the original hanging planter for a reason — it is cheap, it fits almost any pot, it looks good in every style from boho to minimal, and a set of two costs less than a single ceramic hanger. Cotton macrame is also gentle on pots, with no hard edges to chip ceramic, and it folds flat for moving day.

Where it fits: in a window, from a ceiling hook, or from a wall bracket, with a trailing plant like pothos that shows off the hanger's shape. Macrame hangers have no drainage of their own, so keep the plant in a pot with drainage holes and a saucer inside the hanger, or water over the sink and let it drain before rehanging. 2. Ceramic hanging pot with drainage

Why it works: ceramic is the most beautiful hanging planter material — glazed, weighty, and finished in a way plastic never is — and a ceramic hanging pot with a drainage hole and a plug or saucer is also genuinely good for the plant. It holds moisture more evenly than plastic, and its weight keeps it stable, so it does not swing every time someone walks past.

Where it fits: from a proper ceiling hook or a sturdy wall bracket, never from an adhesive hook — ceramic is heavy, and a falling ceramic pot is a broken pot and a damaged floor. This is the pick for a plant you love and a spot you are sure about, like a living room corner with a ceiling joist to screw into, if your lease allows it. Shop ceramic hanging planters with drainage

3. Self-watering hanging planter

Why it works: the hardest part of hanging plants is remembering to water them, because they are out of sight and out of mind. A self-watering hanging planter solves that with a reservoir the plant drinks from over days or weeks, which means fewer watering trips up a step stool and far fewer plants killed by forgetfulness.

Where it fits: in a hard-to-reach spot — high in a window, over a stairwell, or in a room you do not enter daily — where a regular planter would dry out unnoticed. Self-watering planters suit plants that like consistent moisture, like ferns and philodendrons, better than plants that like to dry out, like succulents. Check the reservoir monthly and clean it, or it grows algae. Shop self-watering hanging planters

4. Metal geometric hanging planter

Why it works: a geometric metal planter — a brass or black wireframe holding a small pot — is the modern, architectural answer to macrame. It suits minimal and industrial flats, it is light, and it shows off the plant rather than the hanger, which is exactly what you want when the plant itself is the star.

Where it fits: in a modern living room or kitchen, from a ceiling hook or a wall bracket, with a compact plant like a small fern or a pilea. Metal planters usually hold a separate inner pot, so keep the inner pot's drainage in mind and empty it after watering — a wireframe will not catch drips. Shop geometric metal hanging planters

5. Wooden bead boho hanger

Why it works: wooden beads are boho without the full macrame commitment — a little warmer than metal, a little more structured than cotton cord, and a lovely match for terracotta pots and warm wood furniture. A beaded hanger is also usually shorter than a macrame one, which suits rooms with lower ceilings where a long hanger would dangle into head height.

Where it fits: in a bedroom or a cozy living room corner, with a trailing plant in a terracotta pot. Beaded hangers are light, so they work with adhesive ceiling hooks rated for a few pounds, which makes them one of the more renter-friendly picks on this list. Keep them out of very humid bathrooms, where untreated wood can mark over time. Shop wooden bead hanging planters

6. Glass hanging planter for cuttings

Why it works: a glass hanging planter is not really a planter at all — it is a propagation station, a little glass bulb or bottle that holds water and a cutting while it roots. It is the cheapest way to fill a window with plants, since every cutting is free, and watching roots grow in glass is genuinely fascinating, especially for new plant people.

Where it fits: in a bright window, hung from a suction hook on the glass or a small wall bracket, with pothos, philodendron, or monstera cuttings rooting in water. Change the water weekly to keep it clear, and pot the cutting into soil once the roots are a few inches long — a cutting left in water forever grows slowly and eventually stalls. Shop glass hanging planters for cuttings

7. Woven seagrass hanging basket

Why it works: a seagrass basket is the warmest, most natural-looking hanging planter on this list — it reads as a basket first and a planter second, which makes a room feel softer and more lived-in. It is light, it is cheap, and it suits the warm, cozy palette that makes small flats feel inviting in winter.

Where it fits: in a living room or bedroom, from a ceiling hook, with a full trailing plant that spills over the basket's edge. Seagrass baskets have no drainage, so keep the plant in a plastic pot with a saucer inside the basket, and take the whole thing down to water it over the sink. Do not hang seagrass in a bathroom — constant humidity will stain and weaken the weave. Shop seagrass hanging baskets

8. Tiered multi-pot hanging planter

Why it works: a tiered hanger — two or three pots stacked vertically on one cord — gives you three plants for one ceiling hook, which is the best space-to-plant ratio on this list. It also creates a ready-made display: a trailing plant on top, a compact plant in the middle, and a small upright plant at the bottom, all in one vertical line.

Where it fits: in a corner with a ceiling hook, beside a window, or in a stairwell where a vertical display suits the space. Tiered planters are heavier than single ones, so use a proper ceiling hook into a joist if your lease allows it, and water from the top down, letting each tier drain into the one below — or better, take the whole thing down to water it. Shop tiered hanging planters

9. Lightweight no-drill hanging planter

Why it works: this is the pick for renters whose leases forbid ceiling hooks entirely. A lightweight plastic hanging planter, paired with a strong adhesive hook, gives you a hanging plant with nothing screwed into anything — and when you move, the hook peels off and the planter packs flat. It is not the most beautiful planter on this list, but it is the one that works in the strictest rental.

Where it fits: anywhere an adhesive hook will hold — smooth ceilings, tiled walls, or the underside of a sturdy shelf — with a small, light plant like a young pothos or a spider plant baby. Keep the total weight under the hook's rating, including wet soil, which is heavier than dry soil by a surprising amount. Check the hook monthly, and replace adhesive strips yearly.
