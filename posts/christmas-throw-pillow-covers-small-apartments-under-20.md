Throw pillow covers are the single best-value Christmas decoration for a small apartment, and it is not close. A cover costs a few pounds, folds flat into a drawer in January, and transforms a sofa you already own — no storage unit, no loft, no new furniture. Swap the covers in late November, swap them back in January, and the whole room reads as Christmas without a single other purchase. This roundup picks nine covers under $20 that actually look good in a small space, where one wrong pattern can overwhelm the whole room.

The rule for small rooms is scale: pick one or two statement covers and let the rest be quiet. A big buffalo plaid or a bold reindeer print works as a single accent; four of them on a two-seater sofa looks like a gift shop exploded. Buy covers, not full pillows — your existing inserts do the work, and covers store in a fraction of the space. All prices below are typical for the cover style; check the listing for the size, since a cover that does not fit your insert will look worse than no cover at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What size Christmas pillow cover should I buy?**
Match the cover to your existing insert, or buy one inch smaller than the insert for a plump look — an 18-inch cover on a 20-inch insert looks fuller than a 20-inch cover on a 20-inch insert.

**Do pillow covers or full pillows store better in a small apartment?**
Covers, by a wide margin. Four covers fold flat into a drawer or a zip bag, while four full pillows need a whole shelf or a vacuum bag.

**How many festive cushions should a small sofa have?**
Two to three on a two-seater, with at least one plain or solid cover among them. More than that and the sofa stops being somewhere to sit.

**Can I wash Christmas pillow covers?**
Most cotton, linen, and polyester covers machine-wash cold, but velvet and faux fur usually need a gentle cycle or hand wash. Check the label before December, not after a spill.
