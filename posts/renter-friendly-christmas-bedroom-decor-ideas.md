The bedroom is where Christmas feels most personal, and it is also the room renters are most nervous about decorating. No drilling into the walls, no nails in the door frame, no sticky residue on the paintwork — and in a small bedroom, no room for a full-size tree or a mantelpiece you do not have. It is tempting to skip the bedroom altogether and save the decorating for the living room.

You do not have to. A Christmas bedroom can be built entirely from things that hang, lean, sit or drape, and every one of them comes down in January without a trace. These ten ideas are designed for small rented bedrooms: they use Command hooks, over-door hangers, tension rods and battery power instead of screws and nails, and most of them cost under £20. Start with one or two, and your bedroom will feel like Christmas every night when you climb into bed.

### 1. Stockings on Command Hooks

![Christmas stockings hung from removable hooks on a wardrobe door in a small bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-01.jpg)

No mantelpiece is no problem. A row of stockings hung from the wardrobe door or the side of a chest of drawers looks just as festive as a fireplace display, and it takes five minutes. Use large Command hooks — the kind rated for a couple of kilos — stuck to a smooth door or the flat side of a wardrobe. Space them evenly, hang a stocking from each one, and add a small sprig of faux greenery or a cinnamon stick tucked into the cuff of each stocking for a finished look.

The trick is to keep the stockings light. Fill them with tissue paper rather than heavy gifts while they are on display, and save the real presents for Christmas Eve. In a small bedroom, three stockings are plenty; five will crowd a narrow wardrobe door. When January comes, peel the hooks off slowly, following the pull tab, and the door is exactly as it was.

### 2. A Fairy-Light Canopy Over the Bed

![A fairy-light canopy draped over a small bed in a cosy rented bedroom at Christmas](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-02.jpg)

A canopy of fairy lights turns an ordinary bed into the cosiest corner of the flat, and it needs nothing more than a length of sheer fabric and a string of battery lights. Drape the fabric from two Command hooks on the wall above the headboard, let it fall softly on either side of the bed, and weave the lights through the folds. The glow is warm and low, which is exactly what you want in a bedroom in December — a soft light to fall asleep under rather than a bright ceiling light.

Choose warm white lights, not cool white, and pick a battery pack with a timer so the canopy switches itself off after six hours. A charity-shop sheer panel costs a couple of pounds, is light enough for removable hooks, and folds into a drawer in January. Keep the lights away from the pillow and never leave them on while you are out. ### 3. A Mini Tree in a Basket Beside the Bed

![A small Christmas tree in a woven basket beside a bed in a small rented bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-03.jpg)

A full-size tree has no business in a small rented bedroom, but a mini tree — sixty to ninety centimetres tall — fits perfectly on the floor beside the bed or on top of a bedside table. Stand it in a woven basket or a fabric storage cube instead of a tree stand: the basket hides the pot, looks far better than a plastic base, and doubles as storage for spare blankets once the tree comes down. A potted Norfolk pine works beautifully here, and it can live on as a houseplant after Christmas.

Decorate it lightly. Ten to fifteen small baubles, a short string of battery fairy lights and a simple star are enough — a mini tree overloaded with ornaments looks cluttered in a small room. Keep the colour scheme to two colours plus the green of the tree; terracotta and cream baubles match the warm palette of a cosy bedroom and look far more grown-up than a rainbow of plastic.

### 4. Peel-and-Stick Window Clings

![Peel-and-stick snowflake window clings on a small bedroom window at Christmas](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-04.jpg)

Peel-and-stick window clings are the most renter-friendly Christmas decoration there is: they cling to glass with static, use no adhesive at all, and peel off in January leaving nothing behind. A set of snowflakes, stars or a small winter village scene turns a plain bedroom window into a decoration, and because they sit on the glass they take up no floor space at all — which matters in a small bedroom where every surface is spoken for.

Apply them to the inside of the window so they are protected from the weather, and smooth out air bubbles with a credit card as you go. They work best on clean glass, so give the window a quick wipe first. In the evening, with the lights on inside, the clings glow softly from outside, which makes the whole flat look festive from the street.

### 5. A Wreath on the Bedroom Door

![A Christmas wreath hung on a bedroom door with an over-door hanger in a rented flat](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-05.jpg)

A wreath on the bedroom door is the renter's answer to the front-door wreath: it gives you the same festive welcome every time you walk into the room, and it hangs from an over-door hanger, so there is not a single nail involved. Over-door hangers cost a few pounds, slide over the top of the door, and hold a surprisingly heavy wreath. Choose a slim wreath rather than a deep, full one, so the door still closes properly — a wreath that stops the door shutting will drive you mad by the second week of December.

A simple faux evergreen wreath with a few pinecones and a ribbon is all you need, and it will last for years if you store it in a bag in a cupboard. For the full no-drill method, see our guide to [hanging a wreath with no drilling](/no-drill-christmas-wreath-hanging-renters/).

### 6. Garland Along the Footboard

![Christmas garland draped along the footboard of a bed in a small rented bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-06.jpg)

If your bed has a footboard, a headboard or even just bedposts, you have a ready-made frame for garland — and it needs no hooks at all. Drape a faux evergreen garland along the footboard and tie it in place with ribbon at two or three points, or simply let it rest in the curve of the frame. The garland frames the bed the way a picture frame frames a photograph, and it brings the green of Christmas right into the centre of the room.

Keep the garland light and slim so it does not overwhelm a small bed. A plain green garland with a few wooden beads or dried orange slices threaded through it looks far better than a thick, glittery one, and it matches the calm, cosy palette of a bedroom. Battery fairy lights woven through the garland add a gentle glow in the evening; choose a short string so the battery pack can hide behind the bedpost.

### 7. Battery Candles in Jars on the Nightstand

![Battery-operated candles in glass jars glowing on a nightstand in a Christmas bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-07.jpg)

Real candles and bedrooms do not mix — especially in a rented flat, where a wax spill on a landlord's carpet is a deposit deduction waiting to happen. Battery-operated candles in glass jars give you the same warm flicker with none of the risk, and a cluster of three on the nightstand looks genuinely beautiful. Choose flameless candles with a flickering effect and a timer, so they glow for a few hours each evening and switch themselves off before you fall asleep.

Dress the jars up for Christmas: tie a sprig of faux greenery or a cinnamon stick around each one with twine, and set them on a small wooden tray or a folded piece of tartan fabric. The tray catches any condensation and makes the whole arrangement look deliberate rather than accidental. ### 8. A Present Pile at the Foot of the Bed

![Wrapped empty gift boxes stacked as decoration at the foot of a bed in a Christmas bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-08.jpg)

A pile of wrapped presents at the foot of the bed is pure Christmas theatre, and it costs almost nothing — because the boxes can be empty. Wrap a few cardboard boxes in brown kraft paper, tie them with string or ribbon, and stack them in a corner or at the foot of the bed. They look like gifts waiting for Christmas morning, they take up no more space than a laundry basket, and they are the cheapest festive decoration you will ever make.

Use boxes in different sizes for a natural, abundant look, and stick to two or three wrapping papers in the same palette — kraft paper with red ribbon, or cream paper with terracotta ribbon. Tuck a sprig of dried greenery under the ribbon of the top box for a finishing touch. After Christmas, the boxes flatten and store under the bed.

### 9. A Bedside Hot-Cocoa Tray

![A bedside tray with hot cocoa mugs, candy canes and marshmallows in a cosy Christmas bedroom](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-09.jpg)

A small tray on the nightstand or the dresser, set up as a hot-cocoa station, turns an ordinary December evening into a ritual. Two mugs, a jar of cocoa powder, a small pot of mini marshmallows and a couple of candy canes are all it takes — and the tray itself is the decoration, so nothing needs to be hung or stuck to anything. When friends stay over, it is the first thing they notice; when it is just you, it is the reason you look forward to coming home.

Keep the tray small enough to lift off the nightstand in one move, and use a tray with a raised edge so nothing slides off when you move it. A folded tea towel underneath stops the mugs from rattling, and a tiny vase with a sprig of evergreen makes the tray look styled rather than cluttered.

### 10. Dried Orange and Cinnamon Scent

![Dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks as Christmas scent decor on a bedside table](/assets/renter-friendly-christmas-bedroom-decor-ideas/idea-10.jpg)

A bedroom should smell like Christmas, and the safest way to do that in a rented flat is with dried oranges and cinnamon rather than candles or plug-in diffusers. Thread dried orange slices onto twine to make a short garland for the headboard, tie cinnamon sticks together with ribbon and hang them from a bedpost, or simply fill a small bowl with dried orange slices, star anise and cinnamon sticks and set it on the dresser. The scent is gentle, natural and constant — no flame, no electricity, nothing to remember to switch off.

Dried orange slices are easy to make: slice an orange thinly, bake the slices low and slow until they are dry, and let them cool completely before threading them. The bowl version is even simpler and doubles as a decoration in its own right — terracotta, cream and the warm orange of the dried fruit are exactly the colours a cosy Christmas bedroom wants.

## A Bedroom That Feels Like Christmas

A renter-friendly Christmas bedroom is not a smaller version of a homeowner's Christmas bedroom — it is a different thing altogether, and in a small room it is often the better one. Everything here hangs, leans, sits or drapes, and everything comes down in January without a mark on the walls. By Christmas Eve, your bedroom will be the cosiest room in the flat — and your deposit will be exactly where you left it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I decorate a rented bedroom for Christmas without losing my deposit?

Use nothing that pierces or permanently sticks to a surface: Command hooks, over-door hangers, tension rods, freestanding decorations and battery lights. Peel removable hooks off slowly using the pull tab, and test any adhesive on a hidden spot first.

### Can I hang Christmas lights in a rental bedroom?

Yes, with battery-operated lights and removable hooks or clips. Never use nails, pins or staples, and avoid mains-powered lights draped across the bed. Battery lights with a timer are the safest option for a bedroom.

### What is the best small Christmas tree for a rented bedroom?

A mini tree between 60 and 90 centimetres tall, either artificial or a potted Norfolk pine, stood in a basket on the floor or on a bedside table. It fits a small bedroom, needs no stand, and the potted kind can live on as a houseplant.

### How do I make a small bedroom feel Christmassy on a budget?

Focus on light and scent rather than volume: one string of warm fairy lights, a few stockings on hooks, a mini tree and a bowl of dried oranges and cinnamon cost under £30 together and change the whole feel of the room.

### When should I take Christmas bedroom decor down in a rental?

By the second week of January at the latest. Remove hooks slowly, wipe any marks with a damp cloth, and store garlands and wreaths flat so they are ready for next year.
