## 1. christmas-stocking-holders-no-mantel

Post: https://decorly.homes/christmas-stocking-holders-no-mantel/

🧦 No mantel, no problem — you can still hang stockings in a flat that never had a fireplace.

The stocking is the tradition people refuse to give up, and stocking holders have
caught up with apartment living: weighted stands, no-drill hooks, shelf clamps
and railing holders all hang stockings safely, without a single hole in a wall
you do not own.

Tips from the guide:
• A filled stocking is heavier than it looks — 1–2 kg with small gifts, sweets
  and an orange — so whatever you hang it from must be rated for that weight,
  not just for the empty stocking.
• Weighted freestanding stand: look for a base weight of at least 1.5 kg and
  hooks that curve inward, so a stocking cannot slide off when a child or a
  cat investigates it.
• Adhesive hooks: only hooks rated for 2 kg or more, on smooth, clean paint
  (not textured wallpaper), pressed firmly for 30 seconds — pull the tab
  slowly in January and the wall is untouched.

## 2. small-apartment-winter-entryway-ideas
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-26/pin-winter-entryway-ideas.jpg
URL: https://decorly.homes/small-apartment-winter-entryway-ideas/

Post: https://decorly.homes/small-apartment-winter-entryway-ideas/

🚪 Your small entryway has the hardest job in the flat in winter — wet coats,
muddy boots, dripping umbrellas, hats, gloves and scarves, in a space that
might be one square metre of hallway.

Most entryways fail in winter not because they are small, but because they
were set up for summer, when a single hook and a doormat were enough. Winter
brings twice the gear per person, and all of it is wet.

Tips from the guide:
• Hook rail at two heights — adult height plus a lower row for children, bags
  or dog leads — doubles capacity without taking a centimetre of floor space;
  space hooks 15–20 cm apart so coats do not bunch into one damp mass.
• A boot tray is the single highest-impact winter entryway purchase: a shallow
  tray with a raised lip of at least 2 cm catches snow, rain and grit — empty
  and wipe it once a week, or standing water keeps boots damp.
• Layer your mats: coir outside the door scrapes grit off boots, and a washable
  rug just inside catches the water the coir misses — grit scratches floors
  and water stains them, and two mats catch both before they reach the floor
  you will be judged on at move-out.

All 10 ideas that handle real life: https://decorly.homes/small-apartment-winter-entryway-ideas/

## 3. humidify-houseplants-without-humidifier
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-26/pin-humidify-houseplants.jpg
URL: https://decorly.homes/humidify-houseplants-without-humidifier/

Post: https://decorly.homes/humidify-houseplants-without-humidifier/

🌿 Central heating is the quiet killer of houseplants in winter — and watering
more makes it worse.

A heated flat in January often sits at 20–30% humidity, while most tropical
houseplants — monstera, calathea, ferns, peace lilies — want 40–60%. Crispy
leaf edges and brown tips look like underwatering, so people water more, and
the roots drown while the air stays dry.

Tips from the guide:
• Pebble tray: keep the water just below the top of the pebbles, so the pot
  stays dry — a pot sitting in water trades a humidity problem for root rot.
  Top it up every few days.
• Group plants together: a cluster of five or six plants creates a shared
  pocket of humid air and can raise local humidity by 5–10% — leave a little
  airflow between them so leaves just touch rather than smother each other.
• Mist only the right plants, the right way: morning only, never evening, so
  leaves dry before night; thin, soft leaves only — skip fuzzy leaves and
  succulents. Treat misting as a supplement to grouping or a pebble tray,
  not a solution on its own.

All 9 ways, no humidifier needed: https://decorly.homes/humidify-houseplants-without-humidifier/
