## 1. How to host a Christmas party in a rental without damaging anything
Creative: social/pins/2026-11-02/pin-host-christmas-party-rental.jpg
Pin title (98 chars): How to Host a Christmas Party in a Rental Without Damaging Anything – No-Drill Decor, Deposit Tips

https://decorly.homes/host-christmas-party-rental-no-damage-guide

🎉 Hosting in a rental does not mean skipping the party — it means planning the party around the deposit, so the flat looks exactly the same the morning after.
🌿 Protect the floors before anything else: a washable rug or cheap runner over the high-traffic zones, a wipeable mat by the front door for wet shoes, and coasters or a tray under every drinks station.
🌿 Decorate with nothing that touches the walls except removable adhesive hooks — clean the wall first, press firmly, wait an hour before hanging anything, and pull the tab straight down to remove.
🌿 Keep a small stain kit tucked in a cupboard — clean cloths, soda water and a carpet stain remover — so a spill gets dealt with in minutes rather than discovered the next morning.

## 2. Leftover Christmas tree branches as decor
Creative: social/pins/2026-11-02/pin-leftover-tree-branches.jpg
Pin title (93 chars): 10 Ways to Use Leftover Christmas Tree Branches as Decor – Free Greenery Crafts, Wreath Ideas

https://decorly.homes/leftover-christmas-tree-branches-ideas

🌲 Every Christmas tree leaves a pile of branches behind — and those offcuts are free, fresh, fragrant greenery that will last two to three weeks with a little care.
🌿 Make a mini wreath for an interior door: wire short branches around a small wire frame (or a coat hanger bent into a circle) in overlapping bundles, working in one direction until the ring is full.
🌿 Lay branches end to end along a mantel, shelf or bookcase top, overlapping each one over the stems of the last, for a garland that costs nothing — tuck in fairy lights and the whole display glows.
🌿 Mist a branch garland lightly every few days to keep it fresh, and it will last until the tree comes down.

## 3. Fresh Christmas wreath with grocery store greenery
Creative: social/pins/2026-11-02/pin-fresh-christmas-wreath.jpg
Pin title (95 chars): How to Make a Fresh Christmas Wreath with Grocery Store Greenery – Beginner DIY, Budget Foliage

https://decorly.homes/make-fresh-christmas-wreath-guide

🌿 A fresh Christmas wreath looks like a florist job, but it takes about an hour, costs less than a shop-bought wreath, and hangs from an over-door hook with no trace in January.
🌿 You need a wire wreath frame (usually under $5), florist wire, and sharp secateurs — plus supermarket eucalyptus, pine or fir, and something softer like cedar or olive branches.
🌿 Cut the greenery into pieces about 15 to 20 centimetres long, strip the leaves from the bottom few centimetres, and group them into small mixed bundles: two or three stems of pine, one of eucalyptus, one of cedar.
🌿 Buy more greenery than you think you need — a full wreath uses a surprising amount, and running out halfway through is the one mistake that stalls the project.

🎄 A hallway that welcomes, not blocks — Christmas in the narrowest room of the flat.
🌿 Start with the entry: one hook, one wreath.
🌿 Run the lights along the ceiling line, not the walls, so nobody ducks under them.
