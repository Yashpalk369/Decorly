## 1. small-apartment-fireplace-alternatives
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-28/pin-fireplace-alternatives.jpg
URL: https://decorly.homes/small-apartment-fireplace-alternatives/

Post: https://decorly.homes/small-apartment-fireplace-alternatives/

🔥 No chimney, no mantel, no landlord permission for actual fire — and yet
the feeling a fireplace gives is not really about the fire. It is about a
warm focal point, flickering light, and a place to hang the stockings. Every
idea below recreates that feeling without a single structural change, and
most of them cost less than a nice dinner out.

Tips from the guide:
• A small electric fireplace stove is the closest thing to a real fireplace
  a renter can own: it plugs into a normal socket, produces real heat with
  a thermostat, and the flame effect is convincing from sofa distance.
  Choose a compact model (under 45 cm wide) so it does not eat floor space,
  and skip anything with a fake stone surround — the simple cast-iron-look
  stoves read far more expensive.
• A freestanding faux mantel shelf leans against the wall and needs no
  fixings, yet gives you the whole mantel moment: garland across the top,
  candle clusters at either end, stockings on removable hooks. Measure
  your wall first — mantels come in widths from 90 cm to 130 cm, and one
  that is too wide for the wall looks like furniture that wandered in from
  another house.
• A cluster of pillar candles on a tray is the oldest fireplace substitute
  there is: real flame, real flicker, zero installation, and the tray
  keeps wax contained and the arrangement portable. Vary the heights (at
  least three) and use flameless LED pillars if your lease bans real
  candles or you have pets — modern ones flicker convincingly and run on
  a timer.

## 2. holiday-gift-wrapping-station-small-apartment-guide
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-28/pin-gift-wrapping-station.jpg
URL: https://decorly.homes/holiday-gift-wrapping-station-small-apartment-guide/

Post: https://decorly.homes/holiday-gift-wrapping-station-small-apartment-guide/

🎁 Gift wrapping in a small apartment usually goes one of two ways: it
takes over the dining table for three weeks, or it happens in a frantic
pile on the bed on Christmas Eve. A wrapping station fixes both — it is
one contained spot where every supply lives, every gift gets wrapped, and
the mess has a boundary.

Tips from the guide:
• Choose a spot you can close away: the top of a wardrobe with a folding
  table nearby, a corner of a bedroom with a rolling cart, or a closet you
  can roll a cart into and shut the door on. Measure before you buy — a
  rolling cart is about 45 cm wide and 35 cm deep, and a folding table
  needs roughly 120 cm of wall when open. The station should be reachable
  in under a minute.
• A three-tier rolling cart is the small-apartment wrapping station in its
  ideal form: bottom tier for paper rolls stored upright, middle tier for
  boxes, bags and tissue, top tier for the tools you reach for constantly
  — scissors, tape, pens and tags. It moves to wherever you are wrapping
  and rolls into a closet when you are done.
• Store paper rolls upright, never flat — paper stored flat gets crushed,
  creased and torn at the edges. A tall wastepaper basket, an umbrella
  stand or an upright wrap holder all work. One honest tip: three good
  rolls beat ten mediocre ones — a solid kraft, a classic red or green,
  and one patterned roll cover almost every gift.

## 3. remove-holiday-decor-rental-no-damage-guide
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-28/pin-remove-holiday-decor.jpg
URL: https://decorly.homes/remove-holiday-decor-rental-no-damage-guide/

Post: https://decorly.homes/remove-holiday-decor-rental-no-damage-guide/

🧹 Putting holiday decor up in a rental is the fun part. Taking it down is
the part that decides whether you get your deposit back. The golden rule
of takedown: reverse the order you put things up, work top to bottom, and
never yank anything.

Tips from the guide:
• Photograph everything before you touch it: walk the flat with your phone
  and photograph every decorated wall, window and surface. The photos
  remind you where every hook, nail and adhesive strip is hiding once the
  decor is gone, and they prove the condition of the walls if a landlord
  later claims damage that was already there. Photograph in daylight with
  the flash off so marks show honestly.
• Remove adhesive hooks the way they were designed: for Command-style
  strips, never pull outward — pull the tab slowly straight down along
  the wall, keeping it flat against the surface, until the whole strip
  releases. Pulling outward rips paint, and ripped paint is the classic
  deposit deduction. If a strip snaps, warm the remaining adhesive with a
  hairdryer on low for thirty seconds, then roll it off.
• Take ornaments down top to bottom, starting with the highest wall decor
  too — ornaments and hooks knocked loose from above land on things
  below, so clearing the top first means nothing falls onto a surface you
  have already cleaned. Sort into three piles as you go: keep, repair and
  donate.

The full no-damage takedown guide: https://decorly.homes/remove-holiday-decor-rental-no-damage-guide/

## 5. Store Christmas decor in a rental with almost no storage
Creative: social/pins/2026-10-28/pin-store-christmas-decor.jpg
Pin title (95 chars): 10 Ways to Store Christmas Decor in a Rental With Almost No Storage Space – Small Space Storage

📦 Christmas is over, the decor is everywhere — and your rental has nowhere to put it.
🌿 Under-bed bins take the bulky stuff: garlands, stockings and the tree skirt.
🌿 Vacuum bags shrink soft decor flat enough to slide behind a wardrobe.
🌿 Divided boxes keep ornaments from breaking, and label every bin — nothing unlabelled ever gets found again.
